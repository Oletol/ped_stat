Study Design

DesignSupported conclusionMain limitation
Final measurement onlyShows whether a predefined standard or mastery threshold was reached.Does not show whether the teaching method produced the observed level.
One group measured before and afterShows whether outcomes changed and how large the change was.Time, maturation, other learning and repeated testing remain possible explanations.
One group measured three times or moreShows whether a consistent trajectory develops during the study.There is no simultaneous comparison condition.
Experimental and control groups measured before and afterProvides stronger evidence that the change is associated with the intervention, especially with comparable baselines or random assignment.Validity still depends on group formation, attrition, measurement quality and implementation.

Planning checks

Use comparable instruments before and after learning, record participant-level data, define the significance level and hypotheses before analysis, plan for missing data and avoid choosing a test after seeing the result.

Small samples

Exact or nonparametric methods may be preferable. Report effect sizes and confidence intervals when available. A result above p = .05 may reflect low power rather than absence of an effect.

Results reporting

AvoidPrefer
The method was proven effective because p was below .05.The outcome improved significantly. This result is consistent with the effectiveness hypothesis within the limits of the study design.
There was no effect because p was above .05.The analysis did not provide sufficient evidence to reject the null hypothesis. Interpret the estimate with its confidence interval and sample size.
The correlation proves that X causes Y.X and Y were statistically associated. Correlation does not establish causal direction.
